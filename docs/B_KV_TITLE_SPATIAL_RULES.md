# B KV Title Spatiality Rules — V1.0

## Purpose

Prevent B-stage KVs from collapsing into `photo + flat text` after Stage A has already produced a strong food image.

The title system must participate in the same physical visual world as the product. The rule applies to the main headline and, when present, the subtitle/supporting-title. Product remains visual hero #1; headline remains visual hero #2.

## Hard Rule

A B candidate must not present the headline or subtitle/supporting-title as a flat 2D overlay that could be removed like a pasted Photoshop text layer without materially changing the scene.

The title system must establish visible spatial evidence through one or more of:

- foreshortened perspective or clear angle change;
- extrusion, bevel, relief, layered thickness, or offset planes;
- foreground/midground/background crossing;
- product/type overlap or occlusion;
- embedded, suspended, wrapped, or otherwise depth-bearing carrier;
- contact/cast shadow consistent with the scene;
- shared key light, rim light, reflections, highlights, smoke, particles, or atmosphere;
- scale and plane separation between headline and subtitle/supporting-title.

Literal thick 3D extrusion is **not mandatory for every category**. Restrained editorial categories may satisfy the rule with perspective, layered planes, overlap/occlusion, light integration, and measurable depth separation. The requirement is spatial integration, not one universal 3D style.

## Headline / Subtitle Relationship

When both headline and subtitle/supporting-title are visible:

1. They must not sit on the same flat plane with identical angle, scale logic, and light response.
2. Headline has stronger mass and spatial pressure.
3. Subtitle/supporting-title acts as a secondary depth anchor: shallower/deeper plane, carrier, ribbon, tag, plaque, embedded field, perspective strip, or other category-native device.
4. Their depth relationship must support the product rather than create a giant signboard that demotes the food.

## Anti-Flatness Test

Ask: **If the title were deleted, would the remaining scene look almost unchanged, as if text had only been pasted on top?**

- YES -> `TITLE_SPATIALITY_WEAK`
- NO, because type affects depth, overlap, material, light, or composition -> spatiality can pass

## Production Gate

`TITLE_SPATIALITY_WEAK` is a Production Fast delivery hard gate.

A production candidate with this failure receives at most the normal targeted creative retry. The retry must:

- preserve Stage A reference and product DNA;
- preserve authorized copy;
- freeze already-passing product and composition dimensions;
- rebuild only the title system's depth, perspective, overlap/occlusion, material response, and scene-light integration;
- never solve the problem by shrinking the product or letting the headline become visual hero #1.

## Validation / Evaluation

Independent evaluation must judge visible pixels, not prompt claims. Evidence should identify where the title shows or lacks:

- perspective;
- depth separation;
- overlap/occlusion;
- carrier/material thickness;
- shared scene light and shadow;
- product/type spatial interaction.

Related failure codes remain valid when appropriate: `HEADLINE_WEAK`, `TYPOGRAPHY_DISCONNECTED`, `PHOTO_PLUS_TEXT`, `COMPOSITION_FLAT`. `TITLE_SPATIALITY_WEAK` is specifically for the missing spatial/physical relationship of the title system.

## Forbidden Shortcuts

- flat headline + flat subtitle over a finished food photo;
- headline/subtitle both centered in one planar text block;
- fake depth made only by a generic drop shadow with no scene relationship;
- universal wood-sign / black-gold / neon-sign treatment applied regardless of category;
- oversized signboard or architecture that becomes the hero;
- 3D lettering that forces product shrinkage or masks product identity.

## Acceptance Baseline

A qualified B title system should read as **part of the scene**, not as a late graphic overlay. Spatiality may be bold or restrained, but it must be visible.
